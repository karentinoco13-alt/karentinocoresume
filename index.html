About

Karen Tinoco has spent more than fifteen years leading people and technology inside some of the most demanding organizations in the world: Starbucks, Apple and Amazon. She has run operations across markets, turned around stalled programs, and built the leadership and adoption models that decide whether a new technology is embraced or quietly abandoned.

She holds an M.S. in International Human Resources Management, is pursuing a doctorate in Industrial-Organizational Psychology with an AI concentration, and holds 50 certifications, including AI credentials from AWS, Google Cloud, Anthropic and OpenAI. Her research, including the Human-System Integration Maturity Model (HSIMM), reads AI adoption through the science of how people and organizations actually change.

Born in Nicaragua, she works in English and Spanish across the Americas.

“In an era obsessed with technology, the advantage belongs to the leaders who never lose sight of the people and never stop learning. Learning is key to leading.”

How I lead

On the field or remote, I’m a servant leader who practices Kaizen all the way. Servant leaders roll up their sleeves and go to the Gemba, always inspecting what they expect.

Kaizen: change for the better

It starts with eliminating muda (waste). Waste always gets paid for: in money, in customers who wait, and in people who stop caring because their work no longer seems to matter.

Gemba: go where the work happens

Real problems are found where value-creating work is directly performed, not in a slide deck. Inspect what you expect.

Fix the process, then add AI

Automating a broken process just gets you to the wrong place faster. People, resources, processes and systems should work together, not against each other.
